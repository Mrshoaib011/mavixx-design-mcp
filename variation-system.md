# Campaign variations: recognizable family, distinct members

Read before planning any requested variation batch. Use the delivered graphic as the master, preserve its campaign identity, then give every additional member a deliberate visual or communication role. Transfer principles across all categories; these examples are not instructions to reproduce OPTP artwork.

## Observations from the eleven supplied examples

All eleven files were visually inspected during this update. These are source observations, separate from generation rules. Exact font families are not verified. Source slogans, historic prices and contact details are not current campaign facts.

- Anniversary/flagship set, three portrait images (filenames containing `DHDqSQLsxuD`, `DHDqYcaic0K`, `DHDqbnDq34j`): a shared dark architectural illustration, angular red planes, yellow/white lettering and dramatic perspective connect them. One uses a partial facade and diagonal headline band, another emphasizes a fuller storefront and opening message, and another gives the anniversary numeral/brand sign the main role. The frames have different focal priorities while retaining the same graphic world. These may also read as coordinated neighboring panels; a variation need not become a seamless grid unless requested.
- Pizza set, six square images (the `CSBZ01RCd40` base filename and suffixes `_2`, `_3`, `_4`, `_5`, `_9`): top-left brand anchoring, white display lettering, yellow emphasis, subdued texture and small footer information connect the set. Four product members retain much of the same header/hero/badge/price structure while changing pizza, flavor label and warm brown, olive, ochre or red background treatment. The opening graphic is typography-led; the closing graphic is CTA-led on a dark background. Product substitution is a meaningful difference without moving every anchor. A campaign can mix hero and text-led members.
- Zing Bites pair, two square images (`CQJfhK1gZSl` suffixes `_2`, `_3`): the dark textured surface, yellow brand/price emphasis, top-left brand and footer information unify a product-led composition and a price/CTA-led composition. Their copy dominance changes, while the campaign remains recognizable. Removing a product from a member is appropriate only when the brief permits a text-led member.

## Define the master before drafting siblings

Record the approved visual language, typography roles, hierarchy, decorative-element vocabulary, background/material family, ratio, three color roles and asset identity. Capture the original copy, which headline/supporting blocks may be rewritten, and the actual composition. Lock the CTA wording/button appearance, actual logo and brand/contact/footer elements to their original positions and proportions. Record each fixed element's identity/content, placement and appearance; retain an approved logo asset path when present. Use null for an absent CTA or logo rather than inventing one. Derive these from the actual delivered design and its plan, not a generic named style. Identify a small recognizable signature such as an angled text band, consistent display font with script accent, outlined circles, paper texture or architectural geometry. Preserve this signature across the family without importing these particular examples' identity or food imagery.

Use one of two color policies. Default to fixed HEX roles when a palette is supplied or the original is tightly specified. Otherwise establish a bounded, coordinated set of campaign color variants from the approved brand/template direction before generating the batch. Change a background hue only within this recorded family, retaining type/accent contrast and approximate 60/30/10 balance per image. Keep strict brand palettes and real product/logo colors intact. A background family can vary texture distribution, lighting, graphic geometry or scene staging without changing its aesthetic. Never infer that a brown/red/green source set licenses arbitrary hue changes for another brand.

## Plan meaningful differences without breaking recognition

For each member, assign a purpose: alternate hero composition, detail emphasis, product sibling or message emphasis. Write new headline and supporting creative wording, distinct from the original and every other member, preserving the core meaning and verified factual content. Change the main layout visibly through subject staging, focal scale relationships, headline/copy-zone arrangement or spatial flow. Keep the CTA, logo and fixed footer blocks at their original positions; redesign the main content around those anchors. Pair each change with explicit reasons it remains a sibling (typography, hierarchy, signature elements, palette family). A changed background is optional and must retain the original campaign's theme, material family, palette logic and atmosphere. Do not require three large layout changes or a different thirds mode. Reusing the master template is valid when it suits the new member; alternative templates are subordinate to the locked campaign system.

A product substitution or background change alone does not replace the required new creative wording and main layout. For the same product, create a new staging/composition or emphasis within the shared system, not a tiny nudge, hue-only clone, punctuation/case-only rewrite, decorative speckle change or fictitious camera angle. The standing variation rule authorizes rewriting headline/supporting promotional copy without another permission question. The new resolved copy becomes exact generation text for that member. Keep CTA, brand/product identifiers, price/offer/date facts, contact/website details, legal text and other mandatory factual information unchanged. Rephrasing must not invent claims, urgency, discounts or features. If a different-product request explicitly supplies changed facts, first resolve those verified substitutions in the member's locked fact snapshot; do not apply the old product's facts to the new one. An explicit current instruction to keep exact wording overrides the default and must be recorded. Keep required information in every member unless the user explicitly requests a multi-post narrative that distributes it. Omit the hero only when a text-led member is explicitly allowed; otherwise preserve recognizable subject presence.

For any ratio, reflow the system and its hierarchy instead of stretching or cropping essentials. Record an equal invisible 3x3 grid for every member; it may retain the master's mode with different internal staging. Across all categories, keep the product's real details and original logo, and follow removal/scope decisions. Furniture can vary room framing and type rhythm, beauty can vary subject scale and coordinated textures, and technology can vary feature emphasis and geometric staging without invented product views or facts.

## Composer schema

For every requested additional output, supply `variation` alongside the ordinary brief. Use `mode` of `same_product` or `different_product`, `source_creation_id`, integer `index` and `total` (additional outputs only), a concrete `role`, `master_system`, `family_connections`, and `design_changes`.

`master_system` requires nonempty strings: `visual_language`, `typography`, `hierarchy`, `brand_anchors`, `element_vocabulary`, `background_family`; `ratio` records the locked canvas. `color_variants` is a nonempty list of distinct complete `color_roles` dictionaries (one entry for a fixed palette). Also record `copy` as the original nonempty text blocks, `editable_copy_keys` as the distinct creative headline/supporting roles, `composition` as the actual master composition object, and `fixed_elements` as an anchor dictionary. The anchor dictionary must contain `cta` and `logo`, using null when absent; add footer/contact/brand/price anchors where present. Each non-null anchor requires nonempty `content_or_identity`, `placement` and `appearance`, with an optional actual `asset_path`. Use exact master placement values or descriptive original positions when reliable measured coordinates are unavailable; do not invent pixel measurements.

Copy those fixed anchors unchanged into `variation.fixed_elements`. Default `copy_policy` to `rewrite_creative`. Use `preserve_exact` only with `copy_policy_override` quoting an explicit current user instruction. Put each new member's resolved creative wording in ordinary `brief.copy`, preserving its locked CTA/factual blocks and all required copy roles. Supply `previous_member_copies` with the resolved copy dictionaries for all earlier members in that batch, so a third/fourth member cannot repeat the second member's text. Do not distribute or omit mandatory content without authorization. For verified different-product fact substitutions, resolve the factual snapshot from the new user-supplied details before validation while retaining the actual campaign anchors; keep the original source master and the supplied substitution in task state. Never relabel CTA, identifiers, prices or contact/legal information as editable creative copy.

Each `design_changes` entry has `area` and `change`. Allowed areas: `subject_staging`, `layout`, `copy`, `headline_treatment`, `background_composition`, `campaign_role`, `product_swap`. Require a meaningful `layout` change for every member, while allowing the same thirds mode. Do not use `product_swap` for same-product outputs. When changing the background, add nonempty `background_similarity` explaining the concrete shared theme/material/palette/atmosphere. Resolve all palette choices from the approved master/brief, not these source examples. `family_connections` is a nonempty list of concrete retained signatures.

The composer carries the master typography and language into the generation plan even if another structural template is selected, validates count/mode and palette membership, and appends member-specific generation instructions. It checks plan structure, not whether image pixels actually show diversity or exact type fidelity. Do not claim a visual pass from JSON validation alone. When an explicit instruction revises a locked system, update its master first and preserve that instruction in task state.

## Generate and review the set

Include the approved master image and actual new product assets through the supported image-reference mechanism when available. For explicitly authorized generic concepts or plans-only tasks without a master image, use the recorded written master system; never invent an attachment or claim to have visually compared against an unavailable master. Tell image generation to preserve the master's visual system and follow the member's distinct staging/role; it should not copy the full master composition automatically. Use an intermediate family board as an internal planning aid if helpful, but deliver separate finished graphics. Do not add preview images to the text-only template library. Use compatible members from the single workbook-derived library; do not consult the old separate template/reference collections.

Compare the entire finished set with the original. Check both (1) family recognition through typography, palette logic, recurring motifs, anchors and hierarchy, and (2) member distinction through fresh creative wording and purposeful main-layout changes. Verify that every CTA, logo and fixed footer element retains its original content/identity, appearance, size and position. Confirm that any new background still belongs to the original theme. Ask whether the set looks like one art-directed campaign and whether every member is worth presenting separately. Regenerate repeated-copy members, layout clones, moved anchors and disconnected redesigns. Verify every member's resolved wording, factual meaning, product/logo fidelity, safe margins, contrast and required subject presence. Keep the automatic-template workflow, 0–10% Intelligence Gate, 60/30/10 colors, grid and quantity rules.
